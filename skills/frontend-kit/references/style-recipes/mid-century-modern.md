## School

Graphic poster. The page is a poster that happens to scroll:
composition first, decoration as composition.

## Best for

Poster homages, bold graphic landings, exhibition / event pages,
any brief that wants to be remembered rather than to be quiet.

## Visual language

- Flat colour fields: large areas of a few warm, muted colours
  (ochre, teal, rust, cream) meeting at hard edges. No gradients,
  no shadows, no texture pretending to be a material.
- Bold geometric shapes (circles, arcs, diagonals) as the layout's
  structure, not as stickers on top of it.
- Strong grotesque type at poster scale for the headline, small
  even sans for the details. The size contrast between the two is
  the hierarchy — often four to six times.
- Asymmetric composition: the headline sits off-centre, the image
  sits against it, the empty field balances both.

## Signature moves

- One big shape that IS the hero (a sun, an arc, a diagonal field)
  instead of a hero photograph.
- A limited palette used flat and at scale — the same three colours
  at poster size, at button size, at rule size.
- Tight, confident copy: few words, large; the poster does not
  explain itself.

## Anti-patterns (do not do these in this direction)

- Rounded "friendly" cards, soft shadows, glassmorphism — all
  soften the poster's hard edges.
- A purple-pink-blue gradient (AC-1) — the opposite of flat
  mid-century colour.
- Stock photography as the hero; emoji as icons; a dozen colours
  "for variety" — the palette is few and deliberate, or it is not
  this direction.

## Values

Derive them from the locked reference, not from memory. The exact
palette is the whole system — check each field colour against the
reference screenshots before locking, and verify text contrast on
every colour field that carries type.
