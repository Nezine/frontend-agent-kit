## School

Information architecture, narrative register: a chart is an
argument, and every non-data pixel weakens it.

## Best for

Data narratives, explanatory charts, reports, any page where a
figure must convince rather than decorate.

## Visual language

- Light paper ground, dark ink, serif for the narrative text. The
  chart and the paragraph share one column and one voice.
- Data-ink ratio as the governing rule: remove gridlines, 3D
  effects, shadows, and chart junk until only the data and its
  labels remain. If deleting an element makes the chart clearer,
  delete it.
- Small multiples: the same small chart repeated for each category,
  so the eye compares shapes instead of reading legends.
- Direct labelling on the data (the label sits on the line, not in
  a legend box somewhere else).

## Signature moves

- Sidenotes / margin notes: the aside lives beside the paragraph it
  annotates, not at the bottom of the page.
- A single sparing accent reserved for the one series the narrative
  is about; everything else stays ink.
- The chart's title is a sentence (the finding), not a label
  ("Figure 1: sales").

## Anti-patterns (do not do these in this direction)

- 3D bars, drop shadows, heavy gridlines, rainbow series colours —
  chart junk, all of it.
- A legend when direct labelling fits; a pie chart when a table
  would say it in one line.
- Decorative photography or illustration on a data page — the data
  is the visual.
- Fabricated data: never invent figures. Real data or an honest
  placeholder.
