## School

Editorial / minimalist. Emptiness as a design decision: the object,
not the chrome, is the content.

## Best for

Object catalogues, quiet product pages, household / stationery /
everyday-goods stores where the product range is the story.

## Visual language

- Near-monochrome: one ground, one ink, almost no chromatic colour.
  Colour, where it appears, belongs to the product photograph —
  never to the chrome.
- Small, even type throughout; hierarchy comes from weight and
  spacing, not from size jumps. Generous whitespace is the layout.
- Grid of objects, evenly spaced, identically framed. No hero
  shouting; the catalogue IS the hero.
- No decoration that is not information: no shadows, no gradients,
  no badges, no "sale" stickers.

## Signature moves

- The object on a plain ground, photographed straight, cropped the
  same way every time — the repetition is the brand.
- Minimal navigation: few words, low contrast, out of the way.
- Long quiet scroll; sections separated by air, not by rules or
  colour changes.

## Anti-patterns (do not do these in this direction)

- A loud accent colour, a gradient hero, or a "bold" display face —
  all three contradict the direction's premise.
- Cards with shadows or borders competing with the objects.
- Marketing copy that oversells ("revolutionary", "seamless") —
  the copy here is plain and factual, or it is wrong for the anchor.

## Values
